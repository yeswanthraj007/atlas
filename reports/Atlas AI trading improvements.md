# Atlas should trade news drift, not factors

Atlas gets materially better by narrowing, not widening. Its zero-skill monthly cross-sectional models are the result the replication literature predicts for public price and fundamental factors in the post-2005, net-of-cost era. Its working volatility model is exactly the kind of forecast the evidence says is reliable, but as a risk engine rather than an alpha source. Its news models are the one alpha candidate that matches what the disclosing institutions actually do: BlackRock, AQR and Man Group all describe domain-specific NLP on corporate text, labelled by the subsequent price reaction. The same-day IC of about 0.26 Atlas measured is the "initial reaction" that published studies show is real but untradeable. The tradeable part is a smaller drift lasting hours to a few days, concentrated in small caps and negative news, and it has decayed fast as LLM use spread. The full 2016-2026 news test is therefore Atlas's single most important pending experiment, and it must be designed around timestamps, costs, decay by year and model training cutoffs or it will mislead. Real-time trading has become far more accessible since the Pattern Day Trader rule was retired on June 4, 2026, but the only intraday edge a retail-latency system can plausibly reach is the post-headline news window, not order-book or speed strategies. The research notes behind this report were cut short by tool limits, so several commonly cited facts (latency, spreads, short interest, insider signals, Lazy Prices itself) remain unverified and are flagged rather than asserted below.

## Institutions disclose text-to-return models and research gates, not stock-picking chatbots

The most specific official disclosure comes from BlackRock Systematic, which trained a proprietary model on **more than 400,000 earnings call transcripts covering over 17,000 public firms** plus two decades of market data, to "learn an association between earnings call text and subsequent market reaction", and benchmarked it against OpenAI GPT models on 500 Q1-2024 calls ([BlackRock](https://www.blackrock.com/institutions/en-us/insights/ai-investing)). The same article argues for narrow models fine-tuned on curated data over general-purpose LLMs. AQR's Cliff Asness calls AI "still just statistics" and "evolutionary, not revolutionary", naming NLP as the most-used signal application and ML-based signal weighting as a second, more stable use ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/ai-is-still-just-statistics-says-aqrs-cliff-asness/)). Man Group's AlphaGPT is an agentic LLM workflow that generates hypotheses, writes production Python and evaluates results; it works best in systematic equities and cannot run "unsupervised just yet" ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha)). Press reporting says it had produced **"several dozen" signals approved for live trading** by July 2025, each passing significance tests, risk review and an economic-rationale check ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-deploys-agentic-ai-for-quant-signal-discovery)).

The sceptics are as informative as the adopters. Ken Griffin said in October 2025 that generative AI helps productivity "but for uncovering alpha it just falls short" ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/generative-ai-fails-to-deliver-alpha-for-hedge-funds-says-griffin/)). Bridgewater's $2bn ML fund uses models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity alongside a decade of proprietary tooling, but Greg Jensen warns that LLMs hallucinate and "don't know what greed is, what fear is"; the fund was incubated on a roughly $100m live sleeve before launch, and humans still run risk and execution ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/01/bridgewater-2-billion-fund-machine-learning-decision-making-openai-anthropic-perplexity)). Goldman Sachs's firmwide AI assistant is documented only as a productivity tool ([Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/digital/goldman-sachs-rolls-out-generative-ai-assistant-firmwide-71562.htm)). Two Sigma's only verified disclosure is its acquisition of Hivemind, software that turns unstructured sources into machine-readable datasets ([Two Sigma](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/two-sigma-announces-acquisition-of-hivemind-software/)).

Renaissance publishes nothing. The widely repeated "right 50.75% of the time" line is a Robert Mercer quote relayed through Zuckerman's book, and the roughly 39.9% net CAGR for Medallion is a third-party reconstruction; the fund is reportedly capped at about $15bn ([Quartr](https://quartr.com/insights/company-research/renaissance-technologies-and-the-medallion-fund)). Treat these as anecdote, but the lesson survives: a barely-better-than-coin-flip hit rate pays only with very high breadth, tight cost control and disciplined sizing. That reframes Atlas's 50% directional accuracy on the cross-sectional test: accuracy was never the right yardstick, but an IC of about zero means there is no edge to scale, whatever the breadth.

Three patterns transfer to a solo builder. First, the replicable alpha idea is supervised text models whose labels are forward returns, which Atlas already has. Second, the 2024-2025 shift is from "LLM as signal" to "LLM as research assistant", always wrapped in statistical gates and human review. Third, live incubation before capital (Bridgewater's sleeve) is standard practice. The notes could not verify anything about satellite or card datasets at named firms, model architectures, holding periods, Aladdin internals, or firms such as D.E. Shaw, WorldQuant or Point72, so this report draws no conclusions about them.

## Atlas's flat factor results match a literature where net anomaly returns are near zero

Atlas's monthly models (price factors, SEC fundamentals, ridge and gradient boosting) showing IC about 0 on a frozen 2025-2026 test is not a sign of a bug; it is close to the base rate. Hou, Xue and Zhang found that of 447 anomalies, **64% are insignificant at 5% and 85% at a t > 3 hurdle** once microcaps are neutralized with NYSE breakpoints and value weights, and 93% of liquidity variables fail ([NBER w23394](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394)). Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen push back on the "replication crisis" framing, finding that most factors replicate within 13 themes and work across 93 countries ([NBER w28432](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432)), but the disagreement is about method, not about the size of what is left to harvest. Post-publication decay is large: McLean and Pontiff report returns about 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication across 97 predictors ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)), and Chen and Velikov estimate that after spreads, publication and the modern trading era the **average anomaly nets about 8 bps per month**, with decay of about 93% after costs ([SSRN 3073681](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3073681)). Both of those figures come from search snippets because the primary pages returned HTTP 403; they match the widely cited abstracts but were not read in full. Earnings drift fares no better: Martineau finds PEAD on raw surprises has not existed in large caps since about 2006 ([SSRN 3111607](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3111607), snippet-only).

The ML-specific evidence explains why switching to fancier models will not rescue this sleeve. Gu, Kelly and Xiu show trees and neural nets beat linear models, but the dominant signals across all methods are momentum, liquidity and volatility, available from daily bars ([NBER w25398](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398)). Jensen, Kelly, Malamud and Pedersen show that cost-agnostic ML leans on "fleeting small-scale characteristics, resulting in poor net returns", and that learning portfolio weights with a cost-aware objective gives a better implementable frontier ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/machine-learning-and-the-implementable-efficient-frontier)). Kelly, Malamud and Zhou's "Virtue of Complexity" argues that heavily over-parameterized, strongly shrunk models capture more predictability than simple ones ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v79y2024i1p459-503.html)); its main empirical evidence is market timing, not stock selection, and the notes could not verify Nagel's published critique, so Atlas should treat it as a hypothesis worth one cheap test (ridge on many random features), not a fix. Foundation models are not a shortcut either: zero-shot Chronos-large posts an R² of -1.37% and 51% directional accuracy on US daily returns, and CatBoost still beats every foundation model even before costs ([arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)), while a 2026 study finds gains over a random walk "small and sparse" ([arXiv 2606.27100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27100)).

Where cross-sectional signals do survive, turnover is the deciding variable. Novy-Marx and Velikov find most anomalies with **one-sided monthly turnover under 50%** keep significant net spreads, and a buy/hold band is the single most effective mitigation ([NBER w20721](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721)). The only public-market signal family with strong recent evidence is options-implied: a 2026 working paper covering 2015-2026 finds the call-put implied-volatility spread significant (|t| > 4) in every regime and risk-neutral skewness the most robust predictor (t = -13.1), while the older smirk signal has decayed to insignificance in the AI/mega-cap regime ([arXiv 2608.26115](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26115)). That paper is not peer-reviewed, uses proprietary OPRA data and does not test transaction costs. Paid alternative data did carry real information (satellite parking-lot counts earned 4-5% around retailer earnings in 2011-2017) but the gain accrued to well-funded funds ([Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/how-hedge-funds-use-satellite-images-to-beat-wall-street-and-main-street/)), and no free substitute (Form 4, short interest, Google Trends) had verified recent evidence in the notes.

Atlas's volatility model sits on firmer ground. Realized volatility is genuinely forecastable: off-the-shelf ML beats HAR on 29 DJIA stocks ([CREATES RP 2021-03](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/21/rp21_03.pdf)), but a properly windowed HAR beats ML on 1,455 stocks with the same inputs, and the fitting scheme matters more than the model class ([arXiv 2406.08041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08041)). Among foundation models, only Tiny Time Mixers edges HAR, and an equal-weight TTM plus log-HAR combination is the most robust ([arXiv 2607.05291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05291)). The strong t = 33 result therefore needs one check Atlas has not reported: does it beat a well-specified log-HAR on the same frozen window? Monetizing it as alpha is the wrong move. Moreira and Muir found large in-sample alphas from scaling exposure by volatility ([NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208)), but Cederburg et al. show across 103 strategies that real-time volatility-managed portfolios "do not systematically outperform" unmanaged ones because the parameters are unstable ([JFE PDF](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). The volatility forecast belongs in position sizing, stop distances, exposure caps and order aggressiveness.

## News reads correctly same-day, but the tradeable drift is small and shrinking

Atlas's news result (IC about 0.26 same-day, about zero next-day in a small sample) reproduces the core finding of Lopez-Lira and Tang, who separate a **non-tradeable initial reaction**, which GPT-4 predicts with 88.8-93.3% hit rates, from a smaller **tradeable drift** that is strongest "for smaller-capitalization stocks and negative news" ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619)). The decay is steep: for intraday news, the score coefficient is 0.189 (t = 5.12) on the return from 15 minutes after the headline to the close, falling to 0.116 (t = 2.31) for the next day, and the short leg earns 26 bps/day against 8 bps for the long leg ([Lopez-Lira and Tang v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). Over the post-cutoff period the overnight-news strategy posted a gross Sharpe of 2.97, but it was **profitable at 5-10 bps round-trip and unprofitable at 20 bps**, and its Sharpe fell from 6.54 in 2021Q4 to 1.22 in early 2024 as LLM adoption spread ([Lopez-Lira and Tang v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). A separate high-frequency study finds post-news drift continuing for several days without reversal and surviving costs ([SSRN 2679614](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614)), though that source was seen only in search results. Stale or repeated news behaves in the opposite way, with returns reversing over the next week ([Tetlock 2011, IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v24y2011i5p1481-1512.html), search-only).

Atlas's model choices are reasonable in light of this evidence. Supervised embeddings with a learned head reached a Sharpe of 2.53 versus GPT-4's 2.97 overnight, but small embedding models such as MiniLM managed only 1.42 overnight and 0.79 intraday ([Lopez-Lira and Tang v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)), so the size of Atlas's embedding model matters. Masking company names is backed by Glasserman and Lin, who found anonymized headlines outperformed originals because firm knowledge distracts the model ([arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)). Masking does not solve memorization, though. LLMs recall exact pre-cutoff financial values, prompting and masking fail to stop it, the problem "extends to embeddings", and "post-cutoff, we observe no recall" ([arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765)). Studies of time-stamped models disagree on how much this matters: ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT call the look-ahead bias "modest" ([arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21206)), while DatedGPT measures a significant premium of 26.4 bps per standard deviation and still finds a bias-free Sharpe of 3.20 ([arXiv 2603.11838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11838)). For Atlas, the practical consequence is that any part of the 2016-2026 test dated before the embedding model's training cutoff carries an unknown upward bias. A supervised head trained on returns adds its own risk only if training and test windows overlap, which the frozen design already prevents.

Taken together, this gives a concrete design for the pending full news test. Align every timestamp to market hours so overnight news is traded at the next open and nothing released after the decision time is scored. Target the drift directly (open-to-close for overnight news, 15 minutes after the headline to the close for intraday news, and 1-5 day holds) rather than close-to-close. Report results separately for pre-cutoff and post-cutoff dates, by year to expose decay, by size bucket, and by positive versus negative news. Add a cost sweep at 5, 10 and 20 bps round-trip and a staleness filter based on similarity to the firm's previous stories. Because the alpha concentrates in small caps and the short side, also check spreads and short availability before treating any backtest figure as achievable. The "Lazy Prices" filing-similarity signal is a natural cousin of the staleness logic, but the research notes contain no verified source on it, so its expected strength is unknown and its frozen test should be judged on its own merits.

## Intraday trading is now permitted at $2,000, but only the news window is reachable

The regulatory barrier has fallen. The SEC approved FINRA's Rule 4210 amendments on April 14, 2026; effective June 4, 2026 they remove the pattern-day-trader definition, the day-trade count and the **$25,000 minimum**, replacing them with a risk-based intraday margin standard, with phase-in allowed until October 20, 2027 ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260423-sec-approves-amendments-to-finra-rule-4210-replacing-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-a-modernized-intraday-margin-standard)). The $2,000 minimum for using margin still applies ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)). Alpaca switched on June 4: 4x intraday and 2x overnight leverage from $2,000 of equity, and the `pattern_day_trader`, `daytrade_count` and related API fields were slated for removal by July 6, 2026, with `buying_power` now updating intraday ([Alpaca blog](https://alpaca.markets/blog/finra-retires-the-pdt-rule-introducing-alpacas-new-intraday-margin-framework/)). The new failure mode is the margin deficit: uncovered deficits can freeze credit for 90 days, and Alpaca's docs describe a 90-day restriction on new debits and shorts after five business days unmet ([Alpaca Docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/understanding-finras-new-intraday-margin-rule-and-the-end-of-pdt)). That page also mentions a "12-month" transition that conflicts with FINRA's 18-month phase-in; FINRA's figure is authoritative. Any Atlas code reading the retired fields needs migrating, and every order should be pre-checked against `buying_power`.

Data and simulation limits matter more than regulation now. Alpaca's free plan is IEX-only, with REST data delayed 15 minutes and a 30-symbol WebSocket cap; Algo Trader Plus at **$99/month** adds all US exchanges, real-time REST and WebSocket data, unlimited symbols, and real-time rather than indicative options quotes ([Alpaca](https://alpaca.markets/data)). An intraday news strategy that scans more than 30 names, or needs reliable spreads, effectively requires the paid plan, and so would a daily options-IV collector. Paper trading fills against the NBBO without checking displayed size and does not simulate market impact, latency slippage, queue position, price improvement, dividends or regulatory fees ([Alpaca Docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)), so paper P&L is an upper bound. Extended-hours trading accepts limit orders only, and OPG/CLS time-in-force lets Atlas trade the opening and closing auctions ([Alpaca Docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/orders-at-alpaca)), which suits an overnight-news-at-the-open design.

Explicit costs are negligible; implicit costs decide everything. The SEC Section 31 fee is $20.60 per million dollars of sales from April 4, 2026 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/fee-rate-advisories/2026-2)), and the FINRA TAF is about $0.000195 per share in 2026 per the fee schedule ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rule-filings/sr-finra-2024-019/fee-adjustment-schedule)), though the rulebook page still shows the older $0.000166, so that figure needs re-checking. Alpaca is paid for order flow by Virtu, Citadel Execution Services and Jane Street, so routing is outside Atlas's control ([Alpaca 606](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/SEC+606a1+-+2024Q3.pdf)). The notes verified no 2026 spread figures and no Alpaca latency figures, so the cost model must be calibrated from Atlas's own fills rather than assumed.

Speed-based strategies are out of reach, and the evidence on them is weaker than headlines suggest. DeepLOB reports about 84% accuracy at 10-event horizons on the FI-2010 benchmark, but its own trading simulation uses mid-prices without costs ([arXiv 1808.03668](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.03668)). When the LOBCAST benchmark retrained 15 models on 2021-2022 NASDAQ data, **F1 fell to roughly 48-61%** and "profitability is far from guaranteed" ([arXiv 2308.01915](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.01915)), and high accuracy "does not necessarily correspond to actionable trading signals" ([arXiv 2403.09267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09267)). RL's strongest evidence is in execution, with 27-36% cost reductions over submit-and-leave baselines for large orders ([UPenn PDF](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/papers/rlexec.pdf)), which is irrelevant at retail sizes where a near-touch limit rule captures most of the saving. The one intraday window with published evidence that minute-latency traders can reach is entry about 15 minutes after a headline and holding to the close ([Lopez-Lira and Tang v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). Real-time Atlas is therefore viable only as an event-driven news execution layer, and only if the full news test shows drift that survives the cost sweep in recent years. Even then, it would be harvesting a signal whose published Sharpe had fallen by more than 80% between 2021 and 2024.

## Ranked improvements for Atlas

The ranking weights expected impact on live, net-of-cost results most heavily, then evidence strength, then effort. "Evidence" reflects the notes' own verification status: strong means peer-reviewed and opened; moderate means working papers or snippet-confirmed figures; weak means firm disclosures, inference or unverified.

| Rank | Improvement | What concretely to do | Expected impact | Evidence | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Run the full news test as a drift test | Market-hours timestamp alignment; targets open-to-close, 15-min-after-headline-to-close, and 1-5 day; splits by pre/post embedding-model cutoff, year, size and sign; cost sweep at 5/10/20 bps; stale-news filter | Decisive: tells Atlas whether its only alpha candidate exists net of costs | Moderate (drift and cost results from an arXiv working paper; memorization and anonymization papers opened) | Low-medium |
| 2 | Make the volatility model the risk engine | Benchmark against log-HAR on the same frozen window; use forecasts for volatility-targeted position sizes, exposure caps, stop distances and order aggressiveness; do not rank picks on it | High for drawdown and risk-adjusted return; near-zero as alpha | Strong (HAR versus ML, Cederburg et al.) | Low |
| 3 | Put costs and turnover into every backtest and portfolio | Charge at least half-spread plus slippage per side; hold bands (enter top decile, exit below top tercile); turnover penalty; partial rebalancing; registry ranks on net-of-cost IC | High: prevents shipping signals that die on costs, which is the most common failure mode | Strong (Novy-Marx and Velikov, JKMP); cost magnitudes moderate | Low-medium |
| 4 | Harden the signal registry | Log every trial; require t > 3 for newly searched signals rather than t ≥ 2; add a paper-trading incubation stage that records live-versus-paper slippage; add a decay monitor that demotes signals whose rolling IC fades | Medium-high: guards against multiple-testing false positives and silent decay | Strong for the t > 3 hurdle (Harvey, Liu and Zhu); weak for firm practices (disclosed process only) | Low |
| 5 | Operational safety for the new margin regime | Remove reads of retired PDT fields; pre-trade `buying_power`, per-symbol and daily-loss checks; kill switch using cancel-all and close-all; end-of-day reconciliation; stale-data alerts | Medium: avoids 90-day credit freezes and runaway losses | Strong for the rules; operational design is inference | Low |
| 6 | Event-driven intraday news layer (conditional on rank 1) | News WebSocket, score within seconds, enter about 15 minutes after intraday headlines and at the open (OPG) for overnight news; liquid names first; $99/month data plan; cloud VM | Medium if the drift survives post-2024 costs; zero otherwise | Moderate but decaying (Sharpe 6.54 to 1.22 by 2024) | High |
| 7 | Start collecting options-implied signals now | Daily snapshot of ATM call-put IV spread and risk-neutral skew for the universe; freeze a test once 12 or more months accumulate | Medium, but results take at least a year because history must be built forward | Moderate (one recent working paper with no cost test; older JFQA results from snippets) | Medium |
| 8 | Finish Lazy Prices and extend text to filings and earnings language | Judge on its own frozen test; favor information beyond the headline surprise, since raw PEAD is gone in large caps | Unknown | Weak in these notes (no verified Lazy Prices source; PEAD finding snippet-only) | Medium |
| 9 | Demote the monthly factor models to a benchmark | Rebuild a low-turnover composite on the JKP themes with NYSE breakpoints and value weights, use it as a baseline, and test ridge-on-random-features once; stop iterating on model class | Low: realistic net alpha near zero | Strong that net anomaly returns are small | Low |
| 10 | Use an LLM as research assistant, not signal | AlphaGPT-style hypothesis-to-code-to-evaluation loop feeding the hardened registry | Productivity, not direct alpha | Weak (firm disclosures only, no performance data) | Medium |

Several tempting projects should not be pursued. Deep order-book models, zero-shot foundation models for returns, deep-RL portfolio agents and paid alternative data all fail on at least one of three counts: they need co-location, the edge vanishes on recent data or after costs, or the data costs more than a solo budget allows. One piece of arithmetic sets expectations for everything above. By Grinold's fundamental law (information ratio ≈ IC × √breadth, which the notes did not source-verify), an IC of 0.02-0.03 across 500 stocks rebalanced monthly implies a gross information ratio around 1.5 on paper, before long-only constraints, costs and decay roughly halve it. That is illustrative, not a forecast, but it shows that even a real, small IC matters only with breadth and cost discipline.

## Conclusion

The important shift for Atlas is to stop treating prediction accuracy as the product. Its two clearest results already sort its future. Volatility is forecastable and belongs in sizing and risk. Cross-sectional returns from public data are close to efficiently priced net of costs. What remains open is whether its text models catch the narrow, decaying slice of news that prices absorb over hours and days rather than seconds. That is the same slice the disclosing institutions target with far more data, and it is also why the result has to be measured after the embedding model's training cutoff and after realistic costs, or it will flatter the signal. The PDT repeal makes an intraday Atlas legal at $2,000, but legality is not the binding constraint. The binding constraints are cost, data quality and decay, and the evidence says those favour event-driven, multi-hour-to-multi-day news trades in liquid names over anything faster.

The largest unknowns are ones Atlas can measure better than the literature can. The notes could not verify retail news-feed latency, 2026 effective spreads, Alpaca order latency, or recent evidence on short interest, insider buying or Lazy Prices. A few weeks of small live trades, with each fill logged against the paper fill and the quote at signal time, would give Atlas better cost and latency numbers for its own setup than any paper. Those numbers then become the hurdle every registry signal must clear.
